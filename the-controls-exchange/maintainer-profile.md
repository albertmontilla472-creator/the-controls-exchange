### Purpose, audience and brand

- The project began as a personal library of videos to study at home. Albert now also uses it as a quick reference for finding documents and other BAS information during ongoing learning.
- He made it public to contribute something useful to the BAS community after repeatedly being asked where people could learn BAS. Many people do not realize how much free material already exists.
- The intended benefit is saving search time, helping both new and experienced people, and sending visitors to the original creators. Albert's hoped-for return is community recognition, goodwill and a stronger professional presence, rather than revenue from the directory.
- Keep the project free to visitors, with no access fees, ads, affiliate commissions, sponsorship sales or paid placements. Do not describe it as a legally registered nonprofit.
- Invite useful feedback, corrections and resource recommendations. Encourage visitors to follow the original creators through Recommended resources.
- The Controls Exchange is the professional website name, suitable for LinkedIn. Smarty.ctrl is the more playful social identity. The `ctrl` reference comes from the keyboard's Ctrl key; any future social-brand logo ideas should remain sleek and BAS-related.
- The branding moved from a general HVAC identity toward building automation controls. Keep the current names and original CX branding unless Albert requests another change.
- Albert has described the website as vibe-coded in launch copy. Keep public descriptions of his role honest; do not invent certifications, seniority or qualifications.
- Free GitHub Pages hosting suits the project: visitors should access the public link without Albert running an on-premises web server. The public site repository and separate private project archive serve different purposes.

### Learning scope and selection

- Prioritize practical programming, commissioning and troubleshooting, with Honeywell Optimizer/IRM first and JCI Facility Explorer/CCT second. Preserve Niagara basics and kitControl, Classic Spyder, CIPer and Distech EC-gfxProgram learning as well.
- Keep Classic Spyder distinct from Spyder Model 5/7 and their IRM workflows. Keep CIPer Model 30 and Model 50 distinct; use the correct product spelling, even when an informal request says "Cipher".
- Keep Niagara AX, N4 and N5 in their own folders. Label plans, previews, readiness information and confirmed releases accurately rather than inferring availability.
- General BAS Learning accepts multiple original publishers. BAS advanced is a subject folder, not an SBA-only folder; SBA remains a preferred source. Keep useful older material instead of deleting it to maintain a rolling twenty-video limit.
- The beginner sequence is explicitly for beginners in BAS, with optional Niagara orientation. Keep it modest on Home; experienced visitors can use the tree and top search. Do not make Optimizer/IRM or other vendor application engineering part of the basic starter route.
- Platform contains Niagara platform-specific learning. Software Manager belongs there. Do not restore Application Copier, Station Copier or Platform Administration folders filled with repeated general videos merely to imitate a product menu.
